Custom software for small businesses in Cincinnati, OH

Online stores, booking, customer portals and business apps for Cincinnati and the tri-state, without the platform fees or the five-figure agency bill.

Cincinnati sits on the Ohio River at the center of a tri-state region that reaches into Northern Kentucky and Southeastern Indiana. It has long been home to major consumer-brand and retail headquarters, and around them a dense network of independent shops, makers, restaurants and service businesses in neighborhood business districts across the city.

Many of those businesses sell directly to customers, online and in person, and that is where a lot of their money leaks: platform fees, app fees and transaction cuts on one side, and hours of manual order, booking and customer follow-up on the other. Stackpine builds custom online stores, booking tools, customer portals and business apps that fix both, on one monthly subscription that covers the build, hosting, security, support and ongoing improvements.

Selling online without the platform squeeze

The big e-commerce platforms want a monthly platform fee, app fees on top, and a cut of your transactions. That works well for them as your sales grow, and less well for you. A custom storefront gives you product pages, checkout and an admin panel simple enough for one person to run, with no per-app surcharges and no platform deciding what your store can do.

One of our recent builds did exactly this for an off-road parts retailer: a custom online store with full inventory, checkout and order management, live in weeks and run day to day by a single owner. Read more about our websites and online stores, or our retail and e-commerce page.

And if a standard platform genuinely fits your store and the fees are manageable, we will tell you to keep it. That honest answer costs you nothing.

Booking, portals and follow-up for service businesses

Not every Cincinnati business sells products. For salons, studios, clinics, contractors and other service businesses, the most valuable tools are the ones that take the phone off the owner’s desk:

  • Online booking and scheduling so customers book themselves and get confirmations and reminders automatically.
  • Customer portals where clients check orders, jobs, balances and progress without calling.
  • Intake and triage so every request, from every channel, lands in one place.
  • Follow-up that actually happens. Our RevLane platform tracks declined services and walk-out quotes for shops and follows up by text automatically. The same idea fits any business that quotes work and waits for a yes.

What a first layer looks like

Most owners do not arrive with a feature list. They arrive with a frustration: orders that have to be copied from the website into a spreadsheet, a booking calendar that only works if someone answers the phone, or a promotion that took a weekend of manual entries to run. The first thing we build is usually small and specific, aimed at the job that eats the most hours. A store owner might start with order management, then add inventory alerts and a customer portal. A service business might start with online booking, then add reminders, intake forms and simple reporting. Each layer is added when it is worth it, never all at once.
